How the Multimodal System Works

Combining vision and clinical text into one shared understanding.

Step 1

Vision Encoder ("The Eyes")

Extracts localized feature maps using DenseNet or Vision Transformers. It pinpoints where textures and shadows sit on the scan.

Visual Feature Map
Step 2

Text Encoder ("The Reader")

ClinicalBERT processes patient notes, fever records, and symptoms to understand the clinical context and history.

Clinical Context
Step 3

Multimodal Fusion ("The Brain")

Cross-attention combines both signals: if the text says "cough & fever" and the scan shows an opacity, it correlates them to raise certainty.

Cross-Attention Fusion
Core Fusion Pipeline (PyTorch) 
# 1. Vision & Text feature projection
vis_tokens = vision_encoder(images)       # [Batch, 256, 512]
txt_tokens = text_encoder(patient_notes)  # [Batch, SeqLen, 512]

# 2. Cross-Attention: Clinical text queries image features
fused_tokens, attn_weights = cross_attention(
    query=txt_tokens, key=vis_tokens, value=vis_tokens
)

# 3. Calibrated output with receipts
probabilities = torch.sigmoid(classifier(fused_tokens.mean(dim=1)) / temperature)

Strict Clinical Rules & Guardrails

Built for medical safety — zero hallucinations, honest confidence, and assistant-only role.

1. Zero Hallucinations

Every claim must have a proof receipt. If a finding cannot be traced to the image (coordinates) or the text (direct quote), it is discarded.

2. Calibrated Confidence

No dangerous 100% confidence on poor scans. When images are blurry or symptoms ambiguous, the AI honestly lowers its score.

3. Second Doctor, Not Replacement

The AI acts strictly as an assistant. It uses physician-to-physician phrasing: "Consider evaluating X based on Y" rather than blunt declarations.
